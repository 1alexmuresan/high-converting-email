# Frameworks

Frameworks teach a thinking pattern. They are not templates to fill in.

---

## The four-step spine (default)

Personalization → Who am I → Offer → CTA.

It maps to the order the reader's defenses fire in. Each step answers the question the
previous step raised.

### Step 1 — Personalization

Greeting, one observation or thing in common, a bridge line.

Job: prove you are not a bot. Buy thirty seconds.

Rules:
- One sentence ideal, two maximum. Longer reads as less real, not more.
- Must not signal selling.
- Should segue naturally into the pitch. Test by deleting it — if the email still
  works, the personalization is decoration.
- Include a small voluntary disclosure about yourself where it fits. It triggers
  reciprocity and reads as human.

Bridge lines that work: "Wanted to run something by you." / "Wanted to see if there
was any overlap." / "Think I can help with something." Or cut the bridge entirely.

> Hey Nick, love your channel man. Very no BS, and it helped me get started in
> management consulting. Think I can help you with something and maybe return a bit of
> the favor you've unwittingly done me.

Everything there is cold-readable. Every creator thinks their content is the no-BS one.
"Management consulting" is the voluntary disclosure and is vague enough to apply to any
B2B channel.

### Step 2 — Who am I, and why does it matter

One or two sentences. Identity fused with social proof.

Job: answer "who is this and why should I care?"

The weak version names your job: "I'm a thumbnail designer." That tells them nothing
about whether you can deliver. The strong version names who you've worked with and
what happened.

Shape: *I currently work with [client or client type in their reference group] to
[do thing]. We [specific number result] in [specific timeframe].*

> I currently work with a 10M-sub YouTuber to build his landing pages. We've made 3M
> in the last month alone.

> I make thumbnails for a few creators around 1-2M subs right now.

> I work with a manufacturer out in Georgia, about your size, and just built them a
> system that cut quoting time from three days to four hours.

The reference group matters more than the size of the number. A result from someone in
their industry, at their scale, is worth more than a bigger result from a stranger's
world. Use "we" here deliberately — it implies a team behind the result even when the
rest of the email uses "I."

### Step 3 — Offer

An observation about their situation, then the offer itself: outcome, time, risk
reversal.

Job: answer "what can this actually do for me?"

The observation should be cold-readable. "I think you're leaking money on your landing
page" applies to nearly every business, and nearly every business already suspects it.

Full treatment in [offers.md](offers.md).

> I went through your landing page and frankly you're bleeding money. I'm confident
> enough that a couple of minor changes fix it that I'd bet I could generate at least
> 100k for you in the next 60 days. I'd do it all up front, no strings, and it'd take
> 5 minutes of your time. Only if I hit the 100k would I ask for a cut.

### Step 4 — CTA

One specific ask with the next step pre-decided.

Job: answer "what do I have to do?"

> Would you be open to 15 minutes? I can give you a ring at 3:30 today, or before noon
> tomorrow, whichever's easier.

> Just send me a title and I'll have it back to you in 48 hours.

> Want me to send it over? One click, about 30 seconds to watch.

Every extra round trip after the reply leaks roughly 5% of the funnel. Two or three
avoidable back-and-forths is a quarter of your revenue.

## Worked example, conversational register (default)

This is the register to reach for first. It gives the four steps room to do their job.

```
Subject: your thumbs

Hi Nick, love your channel man. I've watched so many of your vids. My gf says I listen
to you more than I listen to her, lol.

I make thumbnails for a few creators around 1-2M subs collectively. Not here to pitch
you on paying me or anything, just noticed your thumbs are leaving some money on the
table. Idk how much, but imo your CTRs probably aren't consistently hitting 7-8%
because of some minor placement stuff.

I really like your stuff, as mentioned, so putting it on the table here: I'd love to
make you a 100% free thumb for an existing or new video. I do it in 48 hours. You just
send me the title and a couple of bullets and I give it to you at no cost. Why?
Because I want to show you some value up front, and I know you have to bring food to
the table if you want to eat.

No pressure at all. Just let me know and I'll shoot it over.

Imran
```

Roughly 180 words. Every one of the seven principles fires. "CTRs," "thumbs," "subs"
are in-group vocabulary. The girlfriend line is voluntary disclosure. The free thumbnail
is give-first and micro-commitment at once. Note how the length is what *makes* it read
as human. Compress this to 50 words and it becomes a template.

## Worked example, short register (the exception)

Use this only for saturated senior roles who genuinely won't read more.

```
Subject: reply rates

Hey Sarah, saw you're hiring two more SDRs. Congrats.

I run outbound for three agencies about your size. Last month one of them booked 96
meetings in a week.

Most teams hit follow-up fatigue right when new reps ramp. If that's on your radar, I
could show you what we do about it in 10 minutes.

Worth a look?

Alex
```

Roughly 70 words. Hits rapport, social proof, in-group, micro-commitment. Notice it
still cold-reads (the hiring signal, follow-up fatigue) and still carries a specific
proof number. Short does not mean generic.

## Alternative frameworks

Use these when the four-step spine doesn't fit the situation, or as variant generators
for testing.

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution
Pain → consequences → solution + soft CTA. The workhorse for problem-aware but not
solution-aware prospects.

### BAB — Before, After, Bridge
Painful present → ideal future → your product as the bridge. Good for transformation
offers with a clear before and after.

### QVC — Question, Value, CTA
Targeted pain question → brief value → next step. Good for C-suite; qualifies interest
immediately.

### AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
Hook or stat → their specific challenge → proof → CTA. Good for data-driven buyers.

### PPP — Praise, Picture, Push
Genuine compliment → how it could be better → gentle push. Requires a real trigger, or
it collapses into flattery.

### Star-Story-Solution
Introduce a customer → their challenge → the result. Humanizes the pitch when you have
one strong story.

### SCQ — Situation, Complication, Question
Current reality → complicating factor → question. Consultative; mirrors how
professionals brief leadership.

> Your team doubled this year. That usually means onboarding is eating into selling
> time. How are you handling ramp for new hires?

### ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action
Contrarian hook → simple explanation → proof → CTA. Good for analytical buyers who
need evidence.

> Most sales teams measure rep activity. The top 5% measure rep efficiency instead.
> When Acme switched, they booked 40% more meetings with fewer emails. Worth seeing
> how?

### 3C's — Compliment, Case Study, CTA
Agency and services outreach. The case study does the work.

> Big fan of [Company]. We just built an app for [Competitor] that does XYZ. I have a
> few more ideas. Interested?

### Mouse Trap
Observation plus a binary value-prop question. One or two sentences total.

> Looks like you're hiring reps. Would it be helpful to get a more granular look at
> how they're ramping on email?

### Justin Michael method
Trigger or pain → solution hint → binary CTA. One to three sentences, no introduction.
Deliberately polarizing. For top-tier prospects, quote their own words back at them.

### PASTOR
Problem → Amplify → Story → Testimony → Offer → Response. Long-form. Each element can
be spread across separate touches in a sequence.

## Choosing

| Situation | Framework |
| --- | --- |
| Default, services or agency | Four-step spine |
| Saturated senior role, needs brevity | Mouse Trap, QVC, Justin Michael |
| Strong trigger event | PPP, 3C's, four-step |
| Strong single case study | Star-Story-Solution, 3C's |
| Analytical or technical buyer | ACCA, AIDA |
| Consultative, complex sale | SCQ, PASTOR across a sequence |
| Clear before/after transformation | BAB |
| Problem-aware, not solution-aware | PAS |

When generating test variants, pick two frameworks that produce structurally different
emails. Two PAS emails with different wording is not a test.
