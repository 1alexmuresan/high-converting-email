# Offers

The offer is the reason the email exists. Everything before it buys the attention to
read it.

---

## The offer formula

```
                 ROI × Trust that you'll deliver
Conversion  =   ---------------------------------
                    Friction to get started
```

Nobody calculates this. It exists to keep attention on the three things that actually
move the number. If conversion is bad, one of the three is broken, and it's usually
friction.

---

## Numerator 1: ROI

What result are you actually driving?

Requirements:

**Quantified.** No ranges. "20 meetings," not "10 to 20 meetings." A range signals you
don't know, which means you can't guarantee it.

**Unambiguous.** "20 meetings" and "20 conversations" are different promises. A meeting
is a scheduled call. A conversation could be an email reply. Say the thing you mean.

**Time-bound.** "I'll book you 20 meetings" without a deadline means you could deliver
one this year and nineteen in 2065 and still qualify. Time-bounding is also what makes
the guarantee enforceable, which is what makes it credible.

Shape: **[specific outcome] in [specific timeframe].**

### Sizing the number

Size the promise relative to their business, not yours. A $5M/year creator hearing
"$100k in additional revenue in 60 days" is being asked to believe you can improve
their business by about 2%. A business doing $10k/month hearing the same number is
being asked to believe something absurd.

Also: revenue is a much easier promise than profit. Top-of-funnel is easier still.
Promise the thing you can actually control.

Set the promise conservatively relative to what you've proven. If you booked 96
meetings in a week and you promise 10 in 30 days, the gap between promise and proof is
itself persuasive.

---

## Numerator 2: Trust

Trust isn't built in the offer sentence. It's built by everything around it: rapport,
social proof, in-group signals, and authority — the four principles that establish you
could plausibly do this.

The heart-attack test: someone shouts "is anyone here a doctor?" One man says "I did a
holistic medicine certificate in 1992." Another says "I'm an emergency physician."
Same claim, different trust. Your social proof section decides which one you are.

Proof that you've delivered the result *many times* beats proof that you delivered it
once. "Last week I booked 96 meetings for an MSP I've worked with for three years"
carries far more than "I once got a client 20 meetings."

## Denominator: Friction

The most commonly neglected variable, and the easiest to fix.

Friction is everything they have to spend to say yes: money, time, effort, risk,
decisions, cognitive load. A free offer with a mandatory 10-hour workshop is not a
low-friction offer.

Reduce it by:

- **Bounding the time.** "It'll take 15 minutes of your time once at the beginning, and
  we won't need to talk again until all 20 meetings are delivered."
- **Doing the work up front.** "I do all the config myself. You don't need to do
  anything."
- **Pre-deciding the next step.** "How does 3:30 today sound? I can ring you at
  [number], or send a one-click Meet invite."
- **Making the first action trivial.** "Just send me a title." / "Just reply yes."
- **Removing payment risk.** Risk reversal is friction reduction, not a separate thing.

---

## Risk reversal

The prospect should risk nothing. All the risk sits with you.

Template: **I will do [X] in [Y time] or [Z risk mitigation].**

- I'll generate you $10,000 in 60 days or I keep working free until I do.
- I'll get you 20 calls in 90 days or you get every dollar back.
- I'll do all the work up front and only ask you to pay if you like it.
- I won't charge you anything until you get your first 10 paying clients.
- I'll build it and pay for it myself. Only if you like it will I ask you to work
  with me.

Why guarantee at all: you're asking a stranger for a high-ticket commitment with no
established trust. The obstacles to getting them on a call are much higher than most
people writing the email imagine. A guarantee is the thing that cuts through enough to
get the conversation, where you can explain that it isn't too good to be true.

A guarantee roughly triples top-of-funnel at a cost of around 10% of margin from
offers you fail to fulfill. Net, it's strongly positive.

The offer should sound *almost* too good to be true. If it sounds fully too good to be
true, it reads as a scam. If it sounds reasonable, nobody replies.

## Offer library

Real offers, decomposed. Outcome / time / risk reversal.

**Lead generation**
> I'll guarantee you 20 booked sales appointments in the next 60 days or you don't pay,
> no strings. Just say yes and I'll get started.

*Note: "just say yes" campaigns are worn out. Vary the closer.*

**Live chat software**
> I'll build a world-class live chat widget on your site at no cost. I won't charge you
> anything until you get your first 10 paying clients.

No time bound because it's self-liquidating — you don't get paid until the outcome
lands, so the deadline is structurally irrelevant.

**Proposal system**
> I'll build you the same high-converting proposal template that made [Company] over
> $5M in the last two months. I'll do all the work up front, and only if you like it
> will I ask you to work with me. Just say yes and I'll send yours within 48 hours.

Satisfaction guarantee without calling it one.

**SEO audit**
> I'll run a full SEO audit on your site and show you exactly which pages are losing
> you traffic, completely free. Just say yes and it's in your inbox within 24 hours.

**Google Ads**
> I'll build you a Google Ads campaign that I guarantee will generate 50 qualified
> leads in 30 days, or you don't pay a cent. Just say yes and I'll get it live this
> week.

**Content writing**
> Just send us a title and we'll give you a completely free 500-word blog post on the
> topic, no strings attached.

The friction-minimization framework in miniature: the entire first step is sending one
title.

**CRM build**
> I'll build you a world-class industry CRM at no cost. I'll pay for it all myself, do
> all the work up front, and only if you like it will I ask you to work with me. Just
> say yes and I'll send you a link within 48 hours.

**Thumbnail design**
> I'd love to make you a 100% free thumbnail for an existing or new video. I do it in
> 48 hours. You send me the title and a couple of bullets and I give it to you at no
> cost.

**Dental / local**
> We'll get 10 new patients through your door in the next 30 days for full-price new
> patient exams. We do all of it. By the way, last week we generated 109 for another
> practice.

The gap between the promise (10) and the proof (109) is doing the persuading.

## Full assembled offer + CTA

```
I'll book you 20 meetings in 60 days or you don't pay.

I know you're busy, so it'll take 15 minutes of your time over one brief call at the
start, and we won't have to talk again until all 20 meetings are delivered. To be
clear, I'm so confident in this because I do it every day, that if I don't generate you
20 meetings in 60 days you won't pay me a cent.

How does 3:30 today sound? I can give you a ring at 608-299-4393, or send a one-click
Google Meet invite. Just let me know.
```

ROI in the first line. Friction reduction in the second paragraph, twice (time bound,
payment risk removed). Zero-friction next step in the third.

## Common offer failures

- **Vague outcome.** "Grow your revenue." "Improve your pipeline." Unmeasurable, so
  unguaranteeable, so unbelievable.
- **Ranges.** "10–20k." Signals uncertainty.
- **No time bound.** Makes the guarantee meaningless.
- **The call is the offer.** "Hop on a 15-minute call" is a cost, not a benefit.
- **Feature dump.** Ten features lose to one proof point.
- **Unsubstantiated claim.** "300% more leads" with nothing behind it triggers
  skepticism rather than interest.
- **High friction hidden in the fine print.** Free, but requires a 10-hour workshop.
- **Offer mismatched to their size.** The number is either laughable or terrifying.
