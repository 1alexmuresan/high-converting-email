# Personalization

Personalization drives 50–250% more replies. But the mechanism is misunderstood. It is
not proof of research. It's a way of making the reader stop and think *wait, who is
this?* — which buys thirty seconds of attention.

The governing rule: **if your personalization has nothing to do with the problem you
solve, it's just an attention hack.** Delete your opening line. If the email still
makes sense, rewrite it.

---

## Cold reading

Cold reading is a general statement that applies to most of your list but reads as
specific to the individual. Mentalists use it. It works because people don't recognize
themselves as a representative sample.

> Hey Nick, love your channel man. Very no BS, and it helped me get started in
> management consulting.

This works on essentially every B2B creator. Every creator believes their channel is
the no-BS one, because that's why they think it works. "Management consulting" is broad
enough to overlap with any business content.

> Your content's really informative and your thumbnails are decent, but I thought they
> could be more eye-catching.

Every creator thinks their thumbnails are decent-but-improvable.

> I think you're leaking money on your landing page.

Every business has a landing page and most already suspect it underperforms.

Cold reading is the highest-leverage personalization technique because it scales
perfectly and outperforms most AI-generated individual research. You do not need
scraped data to write a strong opener.

### Building a cold read

1. Pick something universally true of your segment.
2. Phrase it as a specific observation about *them*.
3. Attach a mild judgment or preference, so it reads as a personal reaction rather
   than a fact.

"Your content is informative" is a fact. "Your content's really informative and your
thumbnails are decent, but I thought they could be more eye-catching" is a reaction, and
reactions read as human.

## Voluntary disclosure

Give away a small piece of information about yourself. Reciprocal trust. Used in
rapport-building and in interrogation training for the same reason.

> ...it helped me get started in management consulting.

> My girlfriend says I listen to you more than I listen to her, lol.

> I used to run a website agency out in Vancouver, scaled it to about 10k a month.

The detail should be real, small, and slightly unnecessary. Unnecessary is what makes
it read as human rather than strategic.

## The four levels

**Level 1 — merge tags.** First name, company, title. Table stakes. Roughly 5% lift.
Not differentiating. First name alone in a subject line actually *reduces* replies by
about 12% because it signals automation.

**Level 2 — industry / segment.** Industry-specific pains, trends, regulatory pressure.
Scalable via micro-segmentation.

> Most {{industry}} teams struggle with {{problem}}, which usually means wasted effort
> on the ops side.

**Level 3 — role level.** Challenges specific to their role and seniority.

> As Head of Sales, keeping pipeline steady is probably your biggest headache. Your
> RevOps team is small, so you're likely wearing several hats through this scale-up.

**Level 4 — individual.** A specific, timely observation about that person, connected
to the problem you solve.

> Noticed you're hiring 3 SDRs. Sounds like you're scaling outbound fast. Most teams
> hit follow-up fatigue during onboarding.

Level 4 is the gold standard when the signal is real and connects to the problem.
Level 2 combined with a strong cold read frequently beats a weak, disconnected Level 4.

## Research signal stack

| Signal | Where | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recent funding | Crunchbase, LinkedIn, press | "Congrats on the Series B. Scaling the team fast usually creates X" |
| Job postings | LinkedIn Jobs, careers page | "Noticed you're hiring 3 SDRs, sounds like you're scaling outbound" |
| Tech stack | BuiltWith, Wappalyzer, HG Insights | "Saw you're on HubSpot. Most teams at your stage hit a ceiling with X" |
| LinkedIn activity | Posts, comments, job changes | "Your post about X stuck with me, especially the bit on Y" |
| Company news | Google News, press releases | "Congrats on acquiring X. Integrating teams usually creates Y" |
| Podcasts / talks | YouTube, podcast feeds | "Caught your talk at SaaStr on X" |
| Website changes | Manual review | "Your new pricing page caught my eye, curious how it's converting" |
| Their own words | Interviews, posts | Quote them back to themselves. Top-tier prospects almost always respond |

The four -graphic dimensions worth segmenting on: demographic, technographic,
firmographic, and psychographic. Psychographic — what they actually care about — has
the highest impact and the fewest people using it.

## The 3-minute system

For personalization at volume without collapsing into merge tags:

1. Build a research stack of your top 10 buying signals: 5 company triggers, 5 person
   triggers. Rank by relevance.
2. Build a 3×3 template: personalization attached to a problem, the problem you solve,
   one-sentence solution plus low-friction CTA.
3. Write 5 trigger templates — pre-written personalization paragraphs, one per trigger,
   each with a clean segue into the problem.

The rest of the email stays constant. A strong SDR writes a personalized email in under
three minutes.

## Using AI for personalization

AI should not write your email. It writes *variables* that slot into a template you
already know works.

Do not hand a model the whole email. Hand it one sentence and one field:

> Given this prospect's scraped profile, identify their most prominent web property and
> fill the blank: "Hey {{first}}, love your ____, man."

Output: "love your channel," "love your LinkedIn posts," "love your newsletter."

### The casualization layer

The single highest-ROI AI variable. Strip anything from scraped data that a human would
never say out loud.

- "Left Click Incorporated" → "Left Click"
- "The Pacific Creative Group LLC" → "PCG"
- "Palantir Technologies" → "Palantir"
- "Vancouver, British Columbia" → "East Van"
- "University of California, Los Angeles" → "UCLA"

Nobody says "Left Click Incorporated's revenue is going great." They say "PCG's up 33%
this year." Writing the legal entity name is the clearest tell that you scraped them.
Writing the insider version reads like someone who knows them.

Same technique for neighborhoods, schools, and industry shorthand. Models generally
have enough local knowledge to do this reliably.

## What reads as fake

- "I hope this email finds you well."
- "I came across your profile and wanted to reach out."
- Compliments about abstractions: "love how passionate you are about process
  optimization and aligning corporations with diversity outcomes." No human notices
  that. This is the number-one giveaway of an AI-written email.
- Scraped titles pasted verbatim: quoting the full title of their most recent video
  back at them, complete with punctuation.
- Legal entity names, full university names, formal city names.
- "Loved your post!" with no specifics.
- Personalization that's genuinely specific but connects to nothing: "Cool that you
  went to UCLA!" followed by an unrelated pitch.
- Over-personalization to the point of surveillance.
- Personalization longer than two sentences. The longer it is, the less the reader
  believes it was written for them.

---

## The "so what?" test

Read your opening line as the prospect. Ask: *so what? why would I care?*

If the answer is nothing, rewrite it. If the answer is "huh, who is this?", ship it.
