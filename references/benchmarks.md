# Benchmarks, mistakes, and iteration

Use this data to make informed choices, not as a checklist to satisfy.

## Funnel model

500 emails → 100 opens (20%) → 25 replies (5%) → 8 positive (30%) → 4 meetings (50%) →
1 client (25% close). Roughly **0.2% end-to-end** for average performers.

Useful for setting expectations. If someone wants 10 clients a month at average
performance, they need roughly 5,000 emails a month, which means the list and the
sending infrastructure matter as much as the copy.

## Declining effectiveness

Reply rates fell from 7–8% (2020–2022) to 4–5.8% (2024–2025), roughly 15% year over
year. Drivers: inbox saturation (buyers get 10+ cold emails a week and say 20% are
relevant), stricter anti-spam thresholds (Google's complaint threshold is 0.1%), and
the AI flood adding volume without quality.

The consequence: craft matters more, not less. The gap between average and excellent is
widening, not closing.

## Performance levers, ranked

1. **Hook type** — timeline hooks outperform problem hooks by 3.4x on meetings booked
2. **Personalization depth** — up to 250% more replies
3. **Brevity, read carefully** — the 83%-more-replies-under-75-words figure is real but
   segment-blended. It holds hardest for saturated senior roles. For the SMB, creator,
   and trades segments this skill defaults to, Saraev's 120–200 word conversational
   emails outperform clipped ones because they carry the cold read and the guarantee.
   Treat brevity as a lever for the short-register exception, not a universal rule
4. **Targeting precision** — ≤50 contacts per campaign gets 2.76x higher reply rates
5. **Follow-up strategy** — the first follow-up adds 49% more replies
6. **Reading level** — 3rd–5th grade gets 67% more replies
7. **Send timing** — Thursday peaks at 6.87%

Note lever 4 conflicts with the volume requirement for statistical significance (below).
Resolve it by running many tightly-targeted campaigns rather than one broad one, and
pooling the reads across campaigns that share copy.

## Response rates by seniority

- Entry-level: highest engagement, ~8% reply, 50% open
- CTOs / VP Tech: 7.68%
- CEOs / Founders: 7.63%
- Heads of Sales: 6.60% (most targeted role, highest saturation)
- C-level generally: 6.4% versus 5.2% for non-C-suite, but only once the email passes
  their filter

## Industry variation

Highest: nonprofits (16.5%+), legal (10%), edtech (7.8%), chemical (7.3%),
manufacturing (6.1%).

Lowest: SaaS (3.5%), IT services (3.5%), financial services (3.4%).

The low performers are the saturated ones. If the target is SaaS or fintech, the
conversational register and a genuinely distinctive angle matter more than usual.

## Contacts per company

1–2 people per company: 7.8% reply. 10+: 3.8%. Blasting an entire org halves your rate.

## The 15 mistakes, ranked by impact

1. **Wrong length for the register, and too complex to read.** 70% of cold emails read
   above 10th-grade level. The failure isn't word count as such: a 180-word Saraev-style
   email at a 5th-grade reading level outperforms a 50-word one stuffed with jargon. Aim
   the length at the audience (conversational by default, clipped for saturated senior
   roles) and keep the reading level low regardless.
2. **Too self-focused.** "We are a leading..." Count your I/We sentences against your
   You/Your sentences.
3. **No clear value prop.** 71% of decision-makers ignore irrelevant emails.
4. **Generic templates.** {{FirstName}} is not personalization. Recipients detect it
   instantly.
5. **Feature dumping.** One proof point beats ten features.
6. **False personalization.** "Loved your post!" with no specifics is transparent.
7. **Asking too much too soon.** A 30-minute call in the first email is proposing on
   the first date.
8. **Pushy language.** Stacked urgency increases spam flagging by 67%.
9. **No CTA.** Without a next step, momentum dies.
10. **"Just checking in" follow-ups.** "I never heard back" costs 12% of bookings.
11. **Wrong tone for the audience.** A founder, a RevOps lead, and a sales leader are
    three different readers.
12. **Jargon.** "Leverage our synergistic platform" → "we help you book more meetings."
13. **Unsubstantiated claims.** "300% more leads" with no proof triggers skepticism.
14. **Too many contacts per company.** See above.
15. **Fake urgency.** Fake Re:/Fwd:, countdown timers, manufactured deadlines.

## Iteration protocol

Campaigns essentially never work on the first send. The realistic path is 2.5% → 3.2%
→ 3.0% (a bad change) → 4.0% → plateau. The plateau is the campaign's true rate. The
only way to reach it is data.

**Always run more than one variant.** Running a single campaign leaves money on the
table permanently. Most sending platforms (Instantly, Smartlead, HeyReach) support
variants natively.

**500–1,000 sends per variant before deciding.** A decision off 50 or 100 sends is
noise. If one campaign got 1 reply and the other got 0, you have learned nothing. The
one with 0 may well be better.

**Pick a TAM with enough leads.** If the total addressable market is 300 contacts, you
cannot run even one valid test. 100,000 contacts means 100–200 tests, which is enough
to reach the plateau.

**Big changes early, small changes late.** At the start you know the least, so explore
the widest. Week one should be two *fundamentally* different emails: 40 words versus
200 words, formal versus casual, deep personalization versus none. That eliminates half
the search space immediately. Then narrow: a 3x difference, then 2x, then 1x, then
single-word changes to a proven winner.

Two PAS emails with different adjectives is not a test.

**Kill losers fast.** Once you have the volume, cut the bottom performers and build new
variants off the top performer. Don't keep sending a known loser out of sunk cost.

**Schedule the iteration.** Pick one day a week and hold it. Twenty or thirty minutes
every Sunday yields 45–50 iteration cycles a year that nobody else gets. The common
failure is iterating obsessively for a week and then never again.

**Log the changes.** Treat it like a lab notebook: what changed, when, what the reply
rate did. Without a log, you can't tell which change caused which movement.

**Data over intuition.** Copy you're certain will fail routinely hits 15% and generates
six figures. Your priors about your own writing are worth less than a 500-email test.

**Let the market write your copy.** When prospects keep asking the same question on
calls, and your answer isn't satisfying, put the answer in next week's email. That's
the highest-quality copy edit available and it costs nothing.

## What to measure

Build the whole funnel, not just reply rate:

opens → replies → positive replies → opt-ins (link clicks, asset views) → calls booked
→ calls held → proposals sent → closed → lifetime value

Tie LTV back to the specific campaign that sourced it. A campaign with a lower reply
rate but a much higher close rate is the better campaign, and reply rate alone hides
that.
